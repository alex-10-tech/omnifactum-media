More than 1,500 years ago, in Roman Britain, a man called Silvianus lost a gold ring and cursed the thief on a sheet of lead. Centuries later a gold ring turned up near Silchester carved with the suspect's name, and people started calling it "the real One Ring". Is it? With The Rings of Power back on November 11, here are the real stories that shaped Middle-earth, and the truth about that ring.

In this video, we discuss:
- Tolkien the philologist: Finnish, Welsh, Old English and the word "middangeard"
- 1916: Tolkien at the Battle of the Somme, and the friends he lost
- Tolkien's "Atlantis complex", Plato's Atlantis and the drowned island of Númenor
- Three real magic rings: Plato's Ring of Gyges, Andvari's cursed ring and Wagner's Ring
- Beowulf's dragon and the stolen cup
- Durin, Thorin and Gandalf in the Old Norse Völuspá
- The Silvianus curse tablet, the Vyne ring and the National Trust's verdict
